A prediction model of PM2.5 in Central Taiwan

Yu-Chen Lai

Abstract

Due to the growing concern over the health risks posed by air pollution, this study focuses on developing an accurate prediction model for PM2.5 concentration in Central Taiwan. The core objective is to identify the most effective variable selection methods that minimize forecasting errors. This research utilizes recursive updating forecasts to generate predictive values and compares the performance of three variable selection criteria: Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), and Orthogonal Greedy Algorithm (OGA).

The model is validated using both real and simulated datasets. The simulations are conducted via the Parametric Bootstrap Method to repeatedly generate parameterized observations. The model performance is assessed using mean squared error (MSE) across 500 iterations. The results indicate that OGA often achieves the lowest MSE and outperforms AIC and BIC in predictive accuracy, although BIC selects fewer variables. Notably, some seemingly irrelevant variables, when included in the model, contribute to improved forecasts, suggesting further research potential.

Keywords: PM2.5, air pollution, variable selection, Orthogonal Greedy Algorithm, AIC, BIC, recursive updating forecast, parametric bootstrap
